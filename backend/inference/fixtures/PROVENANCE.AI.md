# Scientific input provenance and limits

The four NPZ files are verbatim copies of Ivan's motion-review work at repository revision `2b6dc435b0f73a6f255e50ca795900acfd69cd33`, originally under `science/motion/cases/`. `comet-d6` and `comet-d2` rename the upstream `december-d6` and `december-d2` filenames. The production detector, FITS registration approach, bloom component linking, and moving/stationary visual stacks adapt that same work. Original `ml/` files remain untouched.

`presets.json` records SHA-256 digests and public source pages. `observations.csv` is the original upstream observation/ephemeris table with exact public IRSA FITS cutout URLs; `vesta-horizons.csv` is its independent Horizons comparison. The CSVs and NPZ `expected_xy` arrays are evaluation material. Production reads neither CSV nor `expected_xy`, and a regression test replaces expected positions and adds a contradictory target label while requiring identical complete production results.

All recorded fixture grids have 6.15 arcseconds per pixel, TAN projection, and CD diagonal signs `[-,-]`: declination decreases with increasing image y. The production reader preserves that historical convention. Newly registered native FITS use north-up `[-,+]`. The renderer preserves zero-based image coordinates, and both frame dimensions and coordinate convention are explicit in output provenance. Coordinates are computed from the appropriate WCS rather than expected positions.

The legacy stacks subtract the supplied zodiacal foreground and median sky level and retain OUTLIER pixels; Vesta files also contain BLOOM masks. The default `motion` policy retains OUTLIER with warnings; `science` masks those samples. Saturated/bloom measurements are excluded from ordinary residual detection. Separate bloom tracks use connected-component centroids with at least six flagged pixels, with null residual S/N, null learned score and explicit saturated/non-photometry warnings. Their component area is geometric evidence and is not a flux measurement. This method is necessary to recover the selected saturated Vesta core.

Production requires three independent observations. It sorts actual UTC exposure midpoint dates, deduplicates repeated observation identifiers, rejects conflicting duplicate metadata and duplicate physical midpoints, separates detectors, and rejects mismatched units. Native FITS require IMAGE, FLAGS, VARIANCE and ZODI with matching dimensions, a celestial WCS and consistent DATE-AVG/MJD-AVG metadata. A source-quality flag is allowed; motion mode additionally permits OUTLIER, while all other flags remain masked for residual detection. Registration interpolates signal only where the mask interpolation has at least 99.5% valid support.

Native files remain unchanged. Results retain frame/observation identity, physical UTC/MJD, detector, unit and available release metadata. Public native download links are provided by the Scala retrieval API, rather than accepting arbitrary remote URLs inside Python.

The detector uses a leave-one-out median background, 1.2-pixel Gaussian filter with at least 80% valid support, positive residual threshold of four robust local-noise units, linear linking within 1.4 pixels, and at least three linked epochs. The default speed interval is 3.075–369 arcseconds/hour. A shared-median candidate count is retained as a classical ablation, and each residual candidate publishes its heuristic rank/score alongside its frozen-model score/rank. The Gaussian-filter residual statistic is a heuristic, not a calibrated significance.

The four included fields were selected around two known objects during development. They verify repeatable recovery and coordinate handling; they do not measure survey precision, recall, false-positive rate or independent discovery performance. Unmatched real candidates are unlabeled. Under default preparation the known comet tracks are within 0.61 pixels of the supplied reference positions. Vesta bloom centroids are within 0.39 pixels. Exact model ranks and scientific evaluation are generated separately under `models/real-evaluation.json`; a synthetic-trained ranker can worsen the known target's heuristic rank.

No PSF matching, wavelength-dependent passband correction, absolute flux calibration, orbit fit, source classification, astrometric uncertainty model or catalog identification is performed. Different positions on a SPHEREx detector can sample different passbands, so residual structures can include color/background mismatch. Interpolation and filtering correlate noise. Short, approximately linear tangent-plane tracks are supported; sparse coverage, curvature, saturation, detector artifacts and poor masks can reduce recovery. The moving/stationary PNG stacks are visual checks on the same scale, not formal statistical tests.

Input and output limits are explicit: at most 32 frames; common grids no larger than 512 × 512; NPZ files at most 64 MiB compressed and 160 MiB expanded; no pickled arrays; at most 100 peaks/frame, 100 published candidates, two million linking checks and 50,000 intermediate tracks; PNG resources at most 1 MiB each; JSON at most 4 MiB. Reached peak/candidate display caps mark the search incomplete. Excessive linking work fails explicitly instead of silently truncating the search. Scala supervises total runtime and one concurrent worker.
